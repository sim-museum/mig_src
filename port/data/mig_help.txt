#TOPIC HIDD_INTRODUCTION|Introduction
On-line help is only available for the map screen
1. *.You are in command of a force of 112 aircraft arranged into seven squadrons.
2. In pursuit of your campaign objectives, you will design missions using this map screen.
3. The day is organized into three sessions: Morning, Midday and Afternoon. Missions should be arranged for each session. Up to 96 aircraft can be used in each session.
4. Some sessions will be lost due to bad weather.
5. The full range of Mission Types can be designed. You can either design a mission from scratch personally or just set the overall parameters and let your staff complete the details.
6. You are cleared to fly missions in the following aircraft: F86 Sabre, F80C Shooting Star, F84E Thunder Jet and the F51 Mustang.

#TOPIC HIDD_BATTLESCENARIO|Battle Scenario
You are supporting a ground battle that is being fought on the Korean peninsula. As the peninsula runs north-south you will find that the front line generally lies east-west. Although your specific objectives will vary from campaign to campaign, the overall objective is to support the UN ground forces in their effort to move the front line up to the Chinese border.
It is possible to support the ground forces by flying Close Air Support missions. This may be necessary when UN Forces are in danger of being over-run. However this is not necessarily the best use of your resources. The Communist ground threat depends on getting supplies to the front line. Successful Interdiction missions can therefore delay a Communist advance and even force a retreat.
For the sake of convenience, three Main Supply Routes (MSR) have been identified. There is one on the West Coast, one in the Central Region and one on the East. Each Communist asset has been allocated to a route so that during briefings you will be able to determine its sphere of influence. Often the sphere of influence is obvious; sometimes though, because of the mountainous landscape, the connection is not immediately apparent.
It should be noted that the Supply Routes do not operate independently. There are many cross-links that the Communists use to bypass destroyed bridges etc.

#TOPIC HIDD_RESOURCES|Resources
Your have five fighter squadrons and two bomber squadrons under your command.
The fighter squadrons which are based in Korea, consist of:
Two squadrons of F86 Sabre Jet aircraft. These are the best air combat aircraft at your disposal. Sabres are only armed for air to air duties.
One squadron of F84E jets and one squadron of F80C jets. These aircraft are best suited to ground strike duties. The MiG15 in combat outclasses them.
One squadron of F51 prop aircraft. These aircraft are no match for the MiG15 in combat. However they are useful for Close Air Support and Interdiction Missions close to the front.
The bomber squadrons are based in Japan. On any particular day you will have either a squadron of B29 heavy bombers or B26 medium bomber aircraft available. B29s are preferable because they deliver a bigger punch. However if the attrition rate becomes too high they will be withheld.
Each squadron of pilots is organized into six flights of four. However, the maximum number of aircraft that can be launched at any session is sixteen and therefore only four flights can fly. This gives you the option of resting pilots.
Aircraft are allocated in units of four, i.e. in flights. So, for instance, if you have designed four Strike Missions it would be possible to allocate two flights of Sabres to each mission. Alternatively you could allocate one flight to each mission and the remaining four Sabre flights could be allocated together to form a MiGCAP over MiG Alley.
A flight consists of two elements of two aircraft. The leader/wingman element is the smallest fighting unit in MiG Alley. Although aircraft are allocated to missions in flights, it is possible to allocate duties to individual elements.

#TOPIC HIDD_MISSIONSTRUCTURE|Mission_Structure
Up to 10 completely independent missions can be set up for any particular session.
Each mission can consist of up to six Waves. A Wave is made up of the Strike or Main Duty Group that can be supported by an Air Cover Group and an AAA Group. Each Wave can have its own ToT (Time over Target).
This system gives the flexibility to design either many small missions or fewer complex missions.
Templates or profiles can be produced so that you can easily regenerate missions.
The Mission Structure has been designed to allow you to generate missions that were typical during the conflict. For instance, due to limited fuel capacity, Combat Air Patrol aircraft could only stay on station for about twenty minutes. MiG pilots soon realized this and so they waited until the Sabres started to leave before mounting an attack. In this situation the Sabres were vulnerable because they did not have the fuel to stay and fight and also get home. To combat this tactic Sabres were organized into Waves so that as one Wave was ready to leave another was arriving.

#TOPIC HIDD_ROUTESINTRO|Routes_Overview
A route is a set of waypoints. Initially, each route consists of the same standard set of waypoints:
Take-Off
Rendezvous
Flights from different airfields will join up here
Ingress
Entry into enemy airspace
Initial Point
The Groups split at this point to perform their individual duties
Regroup
The Groups join up again
Egress
Exit enemy air space
Disperse
Flights from different airfields split up
Landing
As it is possible to drag waypoints around the map, Ingress and Egress waypoints are not always strictly associated with the position of enemy airspace.
Aircraft in the same Wave fly together from the Rendezvous to the Initial Point. Each Group then has its own set of waypoints to follow in the Target Zone.
It is possible to insert and delete additional waypoints. However it is not possible to delete any of the standard set. Additional waypoints can be added to both the main route and Target Zone.
Waypoints can be set for individual elements. If you had a mission with two flights of Flak Suppression aircraft, you could give the four elements different routes in the Target Zone. As it is possible to define Main and Secondary Targets, you could authorize eight different targets with just two flights of aircraft.

#TOPIC HIDD_BASES_TITLE|Bases
Display
This Dialogue displays the blue airfields; that is the ones that are controlled by the UN Forces. Your Fighter Squadrons are shown below the airfield at which they are currently located.
The aircraft icons to the right of each airfield indicate the airfields status. The number of icons shows how many squadrons can be accommodated. The colored icons show the slots already in use. A grey icon indicates that the airfield has spare capacity. The prop aircraft icon means that the airfield resources are limited and only prop aircraft can be located there. Prop and jet aircraft can be located at airfields that have jet icons.
Function
This Dialogue can be used to move squadrons from airfield to airfield. A move will take place automatically at the end of the days combat flying. To move a squadron, click on the squadron that you want to move and drag the pointer to the new airfield then release the mouse button. The computer will only allow moves to acceptable airfields. If the pointer is currently over an unacceptable destination then the restricted icon will be displayed.
It is possible to make the Dialogue bigger by clicking at the bottom of the Dialogue and then dragging downwards. More UN airfields will then become visible.
When an acceptable move has been registered, information about the move will be printed next to the moved squadron. Note that the squadron does not move until the end of the day.
Clicking on an airfield will open the Dossier for the relevant airfield. Clicking on a squadron will open the Squadron Dialogue.
Notes
Part of the job of the Commander is to place his Fighter Squadrons at the most suitable airfields. It is safer for them to be kept to the south of Korea. However this reduces their loiter time over the target area.

#TOPIC HIDD_SQUADS|Squadron Information
This Dialogue displays the list of pilots allocated to the Fighter Squadrons. Clicking on the squadron tab at the top of the Dialogue will change the list of pilots displayed.
You manage the pilots of the five squadrons based in Japan. Man Management of the Bomber Squadrons can be left to the Air Commander in Japan. Each squadron can launch up to four flights of four aircraft per day. There are six flights of pilots available and so the fifth and sixth flights dont fly. They are resting.
The pilot lists are displayed according to Flight and Element. Each flight consists of two elements of two aircraft. The flight and element leaders are on the left column and the wingmen are on the right.
Available Aircraft
The number of available aircraft for the squadron is displayed at the bottom of the dialogue.
At the start of a campaign, each squadron will have 19 aircraft available. That means you start with 3 spare aircraft for each squadron. Repair and replacement of aircraft takes time. It is possible then, when things are going badly, that you will not be able to send out the full complement of four flights.
Rotate Flights
The Rotate Flights combo-box is used to determine how frequently the flights are automatically rotated. For instance, if Every 3 days is displayed, then after every three days each flight will be moved up into the place of the flight above: the sixth flight moves into the fifths slot, the fifth flight moves into the fourths slot etc. The first flight is moved into the sixths slot. Automatic rotation can be disabled by selecting Never.
It is possible for you to manually rotate flights by using the dragging and dropping method described below.
Bingo Fuel
This is the minimum amount of fuel that should be available at the Regroup waypoint. During mission planning, you will be warned if this limit is exceeded. During missions, computer controlled aircraft will return home when this limit is reached.
Functions
Flights, elements and pilots can be moved around the squadron by using the drag and drop method. For instance to swap flight one with flight two:
Click on the flight one icon. The pointer should change to a restricted sign.
Drag the mouse pointer over the flight two icon. The pointer should change to a swap sign.
Release the mouse button. The four pilots from flight one will swap places with the four pilots in flight two.
When the Dialogue is first displayed flights five and six are not visible. You can use the scrollbar to bring them into view. However when moving pilots around the squadron it is more convenient to see all the flights at the same time. Clicking on the bottom edge of the Dialogue and then dragging downwards can increase the overall size of the Dialogue.
Click on any pilot to get the personal details of the four pilots in the clicked pilots flight. This information is vital when deciding on promotions, demotions and allocating rest days.
Some of the routine management can be done automatically by setting up the rotate flights combo-box. There are six flights, four of which are on active service each day. By setting the rotate flights to two days, you ensure that your pilots get two days rest out of every six.
Clicking on the Losses button will take you to the casualty lists for all your squadrons.

#TOPIC HIDD_PAINTSHOP|Paint Shop
This Dialogue is launched when you click on an aircrafts nose art that is displayed on the Flight Details Dialogue.
It is possible to change the artwork on any of the aircraft under your command that are based in Korea. You can choose from any of the sixteen pieces of artwork supplied. In addition it is possible for you to provide your own set of sixteen pieces of artwork.
To change the nose art on the selected aircraft, click on your choice.
Changing User Nose Art
In the game the user nose art exists in two forms: one for the map screen and one for the 3D section. Changing the nose art is therefore a two-stage process:
Modify the mapscreen nose art in a standard art pack.
Run a conversion program, which we have provided, to produce the form of the nose art for the 3D section of the game.
Modifying the Mapscreen Nose Art
The source files will be found in: \artwork\noseuser. If you havent changed the default directory the full path will be: \rowan\mig\artwork\noseuser\.
The nose art consists of sixteen files in the bmp file format. You can edit any or all of these files in your art pack. The following restrictions apply:
Each piece of artwork must be 64 pixels x 64 pixels and must have 256 Colors.
The palette cannot be changed.
Color 253 must not be used.
Color 254 is the mask color this will be transparent and not show up on the aircraft.
After saving out a modified piece of nose art, it will be possible to view the change on the mapscreen paint shop dialogue. At this stage, though, the change will not be available in the 3D.
Preparing the Artwork for the 3D Game
To make the modified artwork available in the 3D part of the game, you need to run the nose art program that is installed in the same directory as MiG Alley.
There are two other ways of running the nose art program:
It is available as a menu item in the MiG Alley section of the Start Menus.
It can be initiated from the command line with the command: tt2 r user.res
Running the nose art program will produce a new version of the file: MaskMap\UsrDecal.X8
If at any time any of the user nose art files are corrupted then they can be replaced by copying from the original CD. The directory names on the CD are identical to those on the installed version.

#TOPIC HIDD_WEATHER|Weather
This Dialogue shows the weather forecast for North Korea for the next session.
Conditions/Visibility
This information should be considered when making target choices. Your aircraft are not fitted with sophisticated radar equipment and so target acquisition is by eye. In bad conditions, choose targets that will be easily identified.
Temperature and Pressure
During the summer, it may be difficult for some aircraft with heavy payloads to get into the air.
Contrail Layer
The contrail layer should determine at which altitude you fly. The MiGs have the advantage of being able to fly higher and so generally you should instruct flights to fly below the contrail layer. An attacking MiG is easier to spot as it descends through the layer.
Wind Speed and Direction
Wind speed and direction are quoted at two different altitudes. At certain times of the year it is more likely that the jet stream will offer a tail wind on egress. You should take advantage of this bonus.
Gusts
If the weather is changeable, it is likely that you will suffer from gusts. This will make flying more stressful as you have to constantly adjust the controls.
Special Notes
This gives the forecaster the opportunity of telling you about any special weather effects to note. For instance there may be mist in the valleys.

Category Strike Fighters Targets Missions
This line shows that when 48 Strike and 16 Fighter aircraft are allocated against Airfields, the automatic mission builder found 19 possible targets and was able to use the aircraft allocation to set up 4 missions.

#TOPIC HIDD_LOADPROFILE|Load Profile
A profile holds basic information about a mission such as:
Default waypoints and route
Default type and number of aircraft for the mission
Profiles, then, are very like templates encountered in word processing packages.
When a mission is authorized against a target, you will be prompted to select a mission profile that is appropriate. There are three sets of profiles available:
Standard
User: Strike
User: Patrol
You start with a set of Standard Profiles and these provide a basic mission set-up. The Save button on the Mission Profile Dialogue allows you to save your own profiles. This is useful when you have perfected an attack against a particular type of target and you want to use it on another target of the same type.
After a Standard Profile has been loaded in, the computer a.i. will adjust the number of aircraft allocated to the package to correspond to the size of the target and the surrounding defenses.
After a user-defined profile has been loaded in, the computer a.i. will make only limited adjustments:
B26 and B29 requirements are interchangeable.
The two F86 squadrons are also interchangeable.
In addition, for user-defined profiles:
The computer a.i. never adjusts the number of flights.
Fighter-Bomber squadrons are not interchangeable.

#TOPIC HIDD_WAVEINSERT|Insert a Wave
This Dialogue is launched when the Insert Wave button is clicked on the Mission Dialogue.
You can use this Dialogue to define the main duty of the Wave and the Time over Target (ToT). It is possible to define a Time over Target that would result in the Wave arriving before the main Wave. This could be useful, for instance, if you want to do some flak suppression before your main Wave arrives.
There are three possible types of main duty that can be allocated to a Wave:
Bomb
A mission can consist of a number of Strike Waves. Each Wave can be allocated the main target for the mission or secondary targets can be introduced.
A Crack and Burn mission would consist of two Waves. These missions are useful against substantial warehouses that contain flammable stores. The first Wave would use iron bombs to crack open the store. The second Wave would use napalm to destroy the stores.
Flak Suppression
When an important target is heavily defended, it is reasonable to send out a Wave that arrives before the main strike force to suppress the flak.
Bar CAP
When an important target is located within the MiG area of influence, it is reasonable to send out a Wave that arrives before the main strike force to set up a Combat Air Patrol which acts as a barrier between the strike force and the MiGs.
When the main duty is Flak Suppression or Air Cover, it is still possible to have AAA Cover and Air Cover Groups in support. However this is unlikely to be an effective use of your resources.

#TOPIC HIDD_TASKTABS|Tasks
The Task Dialogue contains detailed information about the aircraft of the current Wave. The details for one Group of the Wave are displayed. Use the Tabs at the top of the Dialogue to view the details of the other Groups.
The first line shows the Squadron Information, for instance B26(1/2). This indicates that the current Group consists of one flight of B26 aircraft. The 2 means that up to two flights of aircraft could be allocated to this group. Click on the box to see what other alternative aircraft could be used for this Current Group.
The next section of the Dialogue is concerned with the detailed orders that can be issued to the Groups. For the main duty it is possible to specify:
Attack Method
Attack Pattern
Group Formation
The Escort Position combo is not relevant.
For the Escort Groups, it is possible to specify:
Radio Silence
Group Formation
Escort Position
The last section of the Dialogue is concerned with giving orders to the individual flights of the Group.
The box next to the Flight designation shows the payload required for the flight. Click on the box to see what other payloads could be defined for this Current Flight.
Extra Targets
The two boxes next to the Target designation show the flights targets. It is possible to define a main and a secondary target for each flight. When a flight is engaged in a patrol duty then these two targets define the extent of the patrol area. Click on a target box to change the allocated target. You will be presented with a Dialogue requesting you to select a target. Click on an item icon on the map to define the target. This Dialogue has two buttons:
Reset
Reset the target to the Missions Main Target.
Cancel
Cancel the request to change the target.
After selecting extra targets, the new waypoints will be detailed in the Routes directory.

#TOPIC HIDD_PAYLOAD|Pay_Load
Details of the Current Payload selection are shown at the top of this Dialogue.
Other options are also displayed and the current selection is highlighted. Click on another option to change the current selection. Click on the tick at the top of the Dialogue to confirm your choice. Clicking on the cross will cancel any changes that you have made.

#TOPIC HIDD_CHOOSESQUAD|Aircraft Select
This Dialogue consists of a list of the squadrons that are under your command. This Dialogue is launched from the Task Dialogue and on entry, the squadron for the Current Group is highlighted.
The number under the Available Column indicates the number of flights that are available for the Current Group. If there is more than one flight available, then the spin button at the top of the Dialogue can be used to change the allocation.
It is possible to change the squadron allocated to the Current Group by clicking on the appropriate line in the list. However, it will not be possible to highlight a line for which there are no flights available.

#TOPIC HIDD_WAVETABS|Routes
See Routes Overview for a description about routes and waypoints in MiG Alley.
Information about the Route for the Current Wave is organized onto two tabs:
Main Route
Target Zone
For each tab it is possible to view the waypoint information in either Summary or Detail Form.
Main Route: Summary Mode
In Summary Mode, a list of all the waypoints on the main route is displayed together with one attribute. Information about the following attributes are obtained by making a selection from the combo-box:
Position
Altitude
ETA
ETA (rel)
Main Duty Fuel
AAA Cover Fuel
Air Cover Fuel
The altitude can be displayed in either feet or meters and the fuel is displayed in either pounds or kilograms. The choice of units can be changed on the Units options on the View set of Preferences. The altitude shown is for the lead group. Escort groups that are of the same aircraft type as the lead group will also fly at this altitude. Other escort groups will fly at 1000ft below the contrail layer.
The ETA is the estimated time of arrival at the waypoint. It is assumed that the Wave will travel at the cruise velocity of the slowest aircraft. The ETA (rel) is the ETA relative to the initial point.
The Fuel figures show the estimated fuel remaining at the waypoint.
When Route information is being displayed in Summary Form, there are three buttons at the bottom of the Dialogue:
Insert
Insert a waypoint after the currently highlighted waypoint. The highlight moves to the inserted waypoint.
Delete
Delete the currently highlighted waypoint. You can only delete waypoints that you have inserted.
Center
Scroll the map so that the highlighted waypoint is at the center.
Main Route: Detail
In Detail Mode, all the attributes for the highlighted waypoint are displayed. In this mode it is possible to change the altitude and ETA for some waypoints:
Altitude
All but the take-off and landing waypoints can be edited.
ETA
Only the ETA of the Initial Point can be edited.
For Detail Mode, there are two more buttons at the bottom of the Dialogue. These allow you to move to the next or previous waypoint in the route. In addition, clicking on the box containing the waypoint description will center the map onto the waypoint.
Target Zone: Summary
The layout is similar to the one for Main Route: Summary.
On the Main Route, the Groups fly in formation. When they reach the Target Zone, they split off and follow their own set of waypoints. There is a tab for each Group so that each set is viewed separately.
The list of Target Zone waypoints always starts with the Initial Point and ends with the Regroup waypoint.
Compared with the Main Route Dialogue, there is an extra combo-box for the Target Zone. The options are:
Group
Flight
Element
You can use this combo-box to view and edit the waypoints at any of the above levels. It is possible then to give orders either at the Group, Flight or Element Level.
For instance, if you want to send out a Group on Flak Suppression, you could just send the Group to patrol an area and let the pilots pick their own targets. However if you know where the targets are and you want to be sure that they are attacked then you can allocate up to two targets to each element.
Instructions on setting extra targets are given in the Task Dialogue.
Target Zone: Detail
The layout is identical to the one for the Main Route.

#TOPIC HIDD_DEBRIEF|Debrief
This dialogue gives further information about the mission and target highlighted on the Mission Results Dialogue. The form of the dialogue will depend on the type of mission.
To get further information about kills and losses, you should go to the Squadron dialogue. Clicking on the Losses button will list the pilots in your command that have been killed. Clicking on any of the pilots on the Squadron dialogue will open the pilot details for that pilots flight. The kills on the last mission are displayed as well as the total kills.
